    How to play
    1. Every curve is cut into 8 equal parts, left to right. Part 1 is shown to you.
    2. For each of the other 7 parts, call what the test error does: up, plateau or down.
    3. A right call reveals that part and moves on. A miss reveals the whole curve and ends it.
    4. A round is 5 curves. Your score is the parts you called right, out of 35.

    Each curve is a power law, an exponential, or one of the seven ill-behaved shapes catalogued in the review. After every curve you learn which one it was.

    The nine shapes come from The Shape of Learning Curves: a Review by Viering and Loog: the two well-behaved forms and the seven ill-behaved cases of its section VI. Section references follow the published version. The curves are drawn from simple formulas, not measured from real learners, and their parameters change every time.
